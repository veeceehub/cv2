Hard Questions

The questions you're
probably already asking.

I'd rather address them here, directly on my own terms, rather than have them surface as unspoken doubts in a room. If a question isn't here, ask me. You'll get the same honest answer.

Your tenure track looks inconsistent, with short stints at Timex and Tynor. Are you a flight risk?+
Look at the full picture before you look at the recent chapters. DY Works: nearly 6 years. Finding ENKI: 6 years I built and ran from scratch. Tupperware: close to 4 years with an internal promotion. That's 16 years of deeply committed, long-term work relationships.

The two short chapters, Timex (18 months) and Tynor (9 months), both have specific, honest explanations. At Timex, I made a values choice. I delivered the work: 35% brand growth, 8X DTC, 500M+ reach, and then chose to leave an environment that had stopped enabling good work. At Tynor, the leader who hired me exited the company within my first month due to a family business dispute. The mandate I was hired for ceased to exist. Bad luck, not bad judgment.

The person who wants a long-term partner will see the real pattern. I stay when the work is meaningful and the people are right. Proven twice, at significant scale.
Why did you leave Timex, a listed company with 16 brands, the peak of your career so far?+
Because I've learned, experientially not theoretically, that leadership culture matters as much as brand prestige. Sometimes more.

I delivered everything I was hired to deliver. The Timex numbers from my tenure are public and verifiable. What I won't do is pretend that a title or a listed-company credential is worth trading your integrity or the joy of the work you do. I watched too many people bear things silently in that environment. I chose not to.

This is not a complaint. It's a value statement. I am selective about culture and leadership, because I know what it produces when those are right, and what it costs when they aren't. The right employer will understand this.
What exactly happened at Tynor? Nine months is a very short tenure.+
Full transparency. I joined because of the founder's son, a progressive leader with a clear vision for building a modern health-tech marketing function. I believed in the vision and in him.

Within my first month of joining, he had a family-level business dispute with his father and exited the company entirely. The elder founder ran a more traditional operation and did not share the vision for CXO-level investment on the P&L. The mandate I was hired for effectively ceased to exist.

In the time I was there, I built a marketing function from zero: website and UX, content production, influencer programs, digital performance, in-store POS, and AI-powered workflows. The work was real. The timing was genuinely, unfortunately wrong.
You've worked across beauty, housewares, watches, healthcare. Where's the depth?+
The depth is in the methodology, not the category. Brand architecture, P&L accountability, building teams from scratch, integrating digital with physical. These transfer. I've proven it repeatedly.

The more interesting argument: the CMO who has only ever worked in one category can't see around corners. I can bring what a D2C-first brand does to a legacy FMCG. I can bring what a premium watch brand's influencer strategy looks like to a healthcare company that's never thought that way.

My deep expertise: brand transformation, building marketing functions from zero, digital-first strategy, P&L ownership, and making marketing legible to a board and a CFO. These are what actually matter at the CMO level.
Is VeeConsult a real practice?+
Five active mandates across sports infrastructure, design, food D2C, AI platforms, and social impact. SporTech Innovation Lab, Enki Studio, Origine Story, NowCollab, and Saanjha Charitable Foundation, each a real engagement with real deliverables.

And yes, I am open to a full-time CMO role. VeeConsult means I'm not idle. A CMO who joins because they need the job will leave when a better one appears. A CMO who joins because it's genuinely the right fit stays and builds.
You say right people matter more than right company. What does that mean in practice?+
It means I do my due diligence on leadership, not just the JD. I will want to meet the CEO. I'll ask how decisions get made and what happens when marketing says no to something that won't work.

What I'm looking for: a leader who respects expertise, a culture where healthy challenge is welcomed, and an organisation where marketing has a genuine seat at the P&L table.

My mentor is Rekha Pamani Gulati, former CEO of DY Works. She's been my professional north star for 20 years, because she built exactly that kind of environment. It's not a unicorn standard. It's a reasonable one. And I know what it produces.

What I'm Looking For

Deliberately selective.
Not indefinitely available.

I'm not applying to every CMO job that moves. I'm looking for a specific kind of setup, where the work is meaningful, the leadership is healthy, and the joy of building something great is possible.

  • A leader I can respect and learn from. Not a boss who needs managing upward, but a CEO or founder with a real vision and the humility to want a strong CMO beside them, not below them.
  • Marketing with a genuine P&L seat. Not a function that does comms after the business decisions are made. I want to be in the room where strategy happens.
  • A canvas that needs building, not just maintaining. I'm at my best when there's a real brand transformation challenge, not just a budget to deploy.
  • Category is secondary. Culture is primary. I've worked across beauty, FMCG, direct-selling, luxury watches, healthcare. I'll work in any category where the above three are met.
